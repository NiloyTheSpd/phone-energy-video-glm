# Subtitle burn-in plan

## How subtitles work in this film

The subtitle is **part of the page**, drawn by `drawSubs(t)` in
`project/index.html` in the style's own grammar:

- a small **cream paper chip** (`#fbf7ee` rounded strip) over a **darker offset
  block** (`#e4d6bd`, +9/+8 px — the colour-block grammar),
- bottom-left, one or two lines of Caveat Medium 40 px, graphite `#43362e`,
- wiped in left-to-right like a colour block (≈0.2 s), words fading in after,
- English only (the film is one language; the style's two-line stack is the
  demo's bilingual layout),
- the bottom ~160 px of every resting composition is kept clear of essential
  content; during punch-ins the chip may pass over edge content briefly.

Because the chip is drawn by the page, **the burned-in subtitles are already in
`out/video.mp4`** — no ffmpeg filter is needed for the standard render.

## The .srt (external/export subtitle)

`project/script/subtitles.srt` is generated by `tools/build_timeline.py` from
**real audio timing** (the measured Kokoro WAVs + faster-whisper word
timestamps), never from estimates:

- 83 cues, each a group of 1–3 narration lines, wrapped to ≤ 2 lines of ≤ 72 chars,
- every cue holds ≥ max(1.8 s, spoken + 0.6 s) — verified programmatically,
  zero overlaps (verified programmatically),
- cue text uses display spelling (digits, symbols) while the voice text spells
  numbers out for TTS.

## If you want a soft-subbed or re-styled version

1. Render the picture with the chip hidden:
   `--q 'clean=1'` on `core/render/video.mjs` (the page also hides the stylus
   with this flag — for a stylus-on, chip-off variant, set `CLEAN` to only gate
   `drawSubs`).
2. Burn the .srt with ffmpeg (approximation of the chip, not the style's
   handwriting):
   ```sh
   ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -vf "subtitles=why-your-phone-gets-heavier-when-you-charge-it.srt:force_style='FontName=Caveat,FontSize=26,PrimaryColour=&H2E3643&,BackColour=&HEEF0E6&,BorderStyle=3,Outline=6,Shadow=2,Alignment=1,MarginLeft=72,MarginV=56'" -c:a copy video_subbed.mp4
   ```
3. Or convert the SRT to ASS and style the offset block with two stacked
   events (a `#e4d6bd` rectangle behind a `#fbf7ee` chip) for a closer match.
